Research

A biopsychosocial approach to ACL recovery

Passing a return-to-sport test is one milestone. Our goal is a patient who moves well, trusts their knee, stays physically active, and keeps their joints and heart healthy for decades. We study each of those pieces and how they connect.

Themes

What we study

Movement

Biomechanics and strength

We measure landing and gait mechanics, quadriceps strength, and rate of torque development in the lab, and increasingly in the clinic with force-sensing insoles. We have shown that these measures differ by sex, age, and graft and that they relate to how patients perceive their knee.

Mindset

Psychological response to injury

Fear of reinjury and kinesiophobia often outweigh strength symmetry in how ready patients feel to return to sport. We study how these factors develop, how they affect movement, and how they shape long-term activity.

Activity

Physical activity after surgery

Many patients are less active long after they are cleared to play. We use wearable devices and the ACL Reasons survey to measure activity and understand the barriers behind it.

Long-term health

Joint and cardiometabolic health

ACL injury raises the risk of early knee osteoarthritis. We are extending this lens to cardiometabolic health, including arterial stiffness and body composition, in young adults after ACL reconstruction.

Registry

ARROW

The ACL Reconstruction Rehabilitation Outcomes Workgroup (ARROW) is a national collaboration of clinical and academic sites that share standardized return-to-sport testing data. The registry now includes more than 4,000 patients across 15 sites.

ARROW data produced the first age-, sex-, and graft-specific reference values for strength, hop testing, and patient-reported outcomes in adolescents after ACL reconstruction (JOSPT, 2023). Those reference values power the ACL Dashboard.

Clinical tool

ACL Dashboard (DART)

Built with the UVA School of Data Science, the Dashboard for ACL Reconstruction Testing compares an individual patient's results to ARROW reference data, filtered by age, sex, time since surgery, and graft type. Clinicians can print a patient report. Patient data is never stored.

Open acldashboard.com

ARROW sites

Fifteen clinical and academic sites across the United States contribute data. The University of Virginia coordinates the registry.

    Program

    SPRINT

    The step from physical therapy back to sport or intense physical activity is a hard one, and many patients make it with little support. SPRINT bridges that gap with intense, small-group exercise for people who had ACL reconstruction or another knee procedure and are beginning that transition.

    Participants complete a pre-program assessment, eight 75-minute small-group training sessions led by two physical therapists, and a post-program assessment. SPRINT is supported by the Jefferson Trust.
