Research program

Recovery after ACL reconstruction is more than a return-to-sport date

Our work takes a biopsychosocial view of recovery. We connect how patients move with how they think and feel about their knee, how active they are, and how healthy their joints and hearts are years later.

01 · Movement

Biomechanics and strength

Landing and gait mechanics, quadriceps strength, and rate of torque development, measured in the lab and with force-sensing insoles.

02 · Mindset

Psychological response

Injury-related fear, kinesiophobia, and readiness to return, and how they shape both movement and activity.

03 · Activity

Physical activity

Device-measured activity with wearables, and the barriers that keep patients less active after surgery.

04 · Long-term health

Joint and heart health

Early knee osteoarthritis symptoms and cardiometabolic health in the years after ACL reconstruction.

Registry

ARROW

The ACL Reconstruction Rehabilitation Outcomes Workgroup is a national multisite registry of more than 4,000 patients. I direct ARROW, which pools return-to-sport testing data across 15 sites to build age-, sex-, and graft-specific reference values.

About ARROW
Clinical tool

ACL Dashboard

The Dashboard for ACL Reconstruction Testing (DART) lets clinicians compare a patient's return-to-sport testing results to ARROW reference data by age, sex, time since surgery, and graft. Patient data is never stored.

Open acldashboard.com
Program

SPRINT

Intense, small-group exercise that bridges the gap between physical therapy and a return to sport and activity after ACL reconstruction or another knee procedure. Supported by the Jefferson Trust.

About

Short bio

Christopher Kuenze, PhD, ATC, FNATA, is an Associate Professor with Tenure in the Department of Kinesiology at the University of Virginia and an Associate Professor, by courtesy, of Orthopaedic Surgery. He is also a consultant at Inova Health. He co-directs the Exercise and Sport Injury Laboratory (EASIL) and directs the ACL Reconstruction Rehabilitation Outcomes Workgroup (ARROW), a national multisite registry of more than 4,000 patients. His research integrates biomechanics, psychological factors such as injury-related fear, and wearable physical activity monitoring to improve long-term outcomes for adolescents and young adults after ACL reconstruction.

Dr. Kuenze has authored more than 130 peer-reviewed publications. He is a Fellow of the National Athletic Trainers' Association, chairs the NATA Research and Education Foundation's Free Communications Committee, and serves as an Associate Editor of the Journal of Athletic Training.
